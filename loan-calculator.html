Flat rate vs reducing balance

Most Malaysian car and personal loans quote a flat rate. Interest is worked out on the full original loan for the whole tenure, even though you keep paying the balance down. Home loans use a reducing balance rate, where interest is charged only on what you still owe.

That is why a 3% flat rate is not the same as a 3% home loan rate. The real (effective) rate on a flat-rate loan is usually much higher, often close to double on a long tenure. Always compare offers on the true rate and the total you repay, not the headline number.

How to compare two loan offers

Look at total repayment, not just the monthly amount

A longer tenure lowers the monthly instalment but usually costs more in total interest. Put a longer and a shorter offer side by side to see the difference in ringgit.

Check what is not included

This calculator covers principal and interest only. Stamp duty, legal fees, insurance or takaful, and any processing fees are extra, so ask the bank for the full cost.

Frequently asked questions

How is a car loan calculated in Malaysia?

Total interest is the loan amount times the flat rate times the number of years. Add it to the loan, then divide by the number of months to get your monthly instalment.

Is a home loan flat rate or reducing balance?

Home loans in Malaysia are normally reducing balance, so interest falls as your balance falls.

Does this work for Islamic financing?

You can use the profit rate in place of the interest rate to estimate payments. Some Islamic products work differently, so confirm the exact figures with your bank.
